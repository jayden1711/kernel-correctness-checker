# Benchmark Results & SOTA Positioning

Consolidated record of every benchmark run, methodology, and finding from
this round of work, for explaining the project to an advisor. All numbers
below are from real runs (Triton kernels on a real GPU via Colab, CUDA
kernels compiled via `load_inline`) -- nothing here is simulated.

---

## 1. Executive summary

**Claim**: a three-layer correctness checker (structural + numeric +
algebraic) catches Triton/CUDA kernel bugs that naive testing and every
benchmarked SOTA method miss, at zero false-positive cost **on the
fixed corpus inputs these benchmarks use**.

> **Scope of the 0%-FP claim — read before citing it.** Every
> false-positive rate in this document is measured on the **fixed inputs
> generated by the corpus harness** (`spec.make_inputs`), not on
> adversarially-generated inputs. It is **not** a claim that the checker
> never flags a correct kernel on any input. Measured 2026-08-20: under
> the adversarial search, which generates inputs specifically to stress
> the checker, the **reference (correct) kernel failed 29 of 80 proposals
> (36%)** for `causal_flash_attention` — 20 of those from an unsoundness
> in Layer 1's `check_kernel_executed` on shift-invariant operators.
>
> **Updated 2026-08-21 — that unsoundness is fixed, and the rate is
> re-measured.** `check_kernel_executed` now uses a probe ladder (§6.1 of
> `SESSION_HANDOFF.md`). A fresh search under the identical configuration
> puts the false-positive rate on **valid** adversarial inputs at **0 of 58
> = 0.0%**, down from 20 of 71 = 28.2%, with `kernel_executed` contributing
> **0** (was 20).
>
> **A first pass at this reported 17.1% and that figure was wrong** — it
> counted 12 crashes on *out-of-domain* input (rank ≠ 2, or a head
> dimension that is not a power of two, both of which the reference kernel
> cannot accept) as checker false positives. Two Layer-1 checks,
> `check_nan_inf` and `check_dtype_preserved`, return a plain failure for
> **any** exception, conflating "ran and produced a bad number" with
> "could not run at all". Full mechanism and evidence in **§8.3.1**.
>
> **The scope caveat still stands, for a different reason than before.**
> The 0.0% is measured on 58 adversarial proposals for **one** operator, so
> it is not a claim about every operator or every input — cite it **with its
> distribution attached**. What changed is that the adversarial and corpus
> figures no longer disagree: the earlier tension was an artifact of the
> misattribution, not a real divergence. See §8.3/§8.3.1 and
> `benchmarks/CHECK_ABLATION_FINDINGS.md` §3.0.

**Headline evidence**:

| Benchmark | Kernel format | Operators | Mutants | Catch rate | False-positive rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| TritonBench | Triton | 29 | 40 | **100%** | **0%** |
| KernelBench | raw CUDA | 15 | 15 | **93.3%** | **0%** |
| **Combined** | both | **44** | **55** | **98.2%** | **0%** |

No SOTA baseline benchmarked against comes close on both axes at once --
see Section 4. The one miss (of 55) has a fully traced, understood cause,
not an unexplained gap (Section 5.3).

## 2. What the checker is

Three layers, run in order, a kernel must pass all three to be verified.
**Reordered 2026-08-20 so the cheap layers run first** (see the note below):

1. **Structural** -- does the kernel actually run, is the output the
   right shape/type, is it deterministic, did every output element
   actually get written. Includes AST-level static analysis (detects
   ghost-optimization, dead-branch kernel bypass, timing manipulation)
   specific to Triton's `kernel[grid](...)` launch syntax.
2. **Algebraic** -- does the output obey the math it's supposed to obey
   (softmax rows sum to 1, layernorm has zero mean, matmul is
   distributive, etc.). ~100+ individual checks across 29 operator specs.
3. **Numeric** -- does the output match a trusted reference, using an
   **adaptive tolerance** derived from the reference's own measured
   sensitivity to a tiny input perturbation, not a fixed constant. Plus
   per-operator hand-designed adversarial inputs (~5 per operator) and a
   cross-shape sweep.

> **Why numeric moved last, and what it is and is not worth.** Numeric is the
> expensive layer -- warm p50 **15.71ms** against algebraic **1.17ms** and
> structural **3.97ms** -- and the layers short-circuit, so running it last
> means it is only paid for when neither cheap layer caught the bug.
>
> This is **verdict-preserving, not accuracy-changing**: the catch sets are
> nested (structural 4 of 40 mutants ⊂ algebraic 18 ⊂ numeric 40), so anything
> algebraic catches, numeric would have caught too. Reordering changes which
> layer *reports* a catch, never whether there is one. Asserted in
> `tests/instrumentation/check_layer_order.py`, which fails if that containment
> ever breaks.
>
> **The speedup is bounded and mix-dependent — do not quote it without the
> trial mix.** Modelled on this benchmark's 240 trials (40 mutant + 200
> reference): the 14 mutants caught by algebraic-but-not-structural get **74%
> faster** (19.68 → 5.14ms), the 22 numeric-only mutants get marginally
> *slower* (+1.17ms, they now pay algebraic first), and the **200 reference
> trials are completely unaffected** — a correct kernel passes every layer, so
> nothing short-circuits and order is irrelevant. Net on this corpus: about
> **−2.6%**. A workload dominated by buggy candidates would gain much more; one
> validating mostly-correct kernels gains ~nothing.
>
> **MEASURED 2026-08-21 (warm, same timer scope, only the order differs):**
> corpus total **6.750s → 6.553s = −2.9%**, against the −2.6% model. Verdicts
> identical across all four `your_checker` systems.
>
> **But treat that −2.9% as consistent-with, NOT confirmed.** The three
> single-layer ablations should be untouched by a reorder — each runs one
> layer unconditionally — and they moved **+7.2% / +6.1% / −2.7%** between the
> same two runs. Run-to-run noise is therefore larger than the effect, so this
> measurement cannot distinguish −2.9% from zero. It is underpowered by
> construction: 200 of 240 trials are reference kernels that the reorder
> cannot help.
>
> **What IS confirmed, and does not depend on timing:** of the 14 mutants
> caught by algebraic-but-not-structural, numeric checks ran on **14/14
> before** the reorder and **2/14 after**. The short-circuit does what it was
> built to do; 12 of 14 now skip the expensive layer entirely.

**The one-sentence differentiator**: every SOTA method benchmarked here
uses a *fixed* numeric tolerance; this checker's numeric layer uses an
*adaptive* one scaled to the reference kernel's own noise floor. That
single design choice is the direct mechanism behind the false-positive
gap in every table below.

## 3. Current SOTA landscape

Condensed from a full literature sweep (`SOTA_research.MD` has the
complete version with arXiv IDs and quotes). What's out there, and how
this project relates to each:

| Method | What it is | Relationship to this project |
|---|---|---|
| **AutoKernel** (arXiv 2603.21331) | 5-stage numerical gate: smoke test, shape sweep, adversarial-stability inputs, determinism, compile check. The strongest existing multi-stage *numerical* gate. | Directly re-implemented and benchmarked (`autokernel_gate`). |
| **gpuemu / Sarkar** (arXiv 2606.27396, +2606.20128, 2607.02541) | Switchable-knob fuzzer (shape/dtype/value-distribution), 26-op corpus. Reports boundary-shape strategy at 78% recall/0% FP vs. adversarial-value at 99% recall/94% FP. | Directly re-implemented as two systems, matching the paper's own reported tradeoff structure. |
| **Propilot** (arXiv 2606.06747) | LLM-driven algebraic property-skeleton testing (commutativity, identity, permutation invariance, decomposition), 212 TVM operators, 4,579 generated tests. Most rigorously evaluated prior art on the algebraic layer specifically -- but compiler-scoped (TVM), not kernel-scoped. | Directly re-implemented (4 generic skeletons, applicability-gated to 5 of this project's 29 operators -- an honest partial coverage, not a full clone). |
| **KernelBench** (ICML'25, arXiv 2502.10517) | Benchmark for "can LLMs write efficient GPU kernels" -- 250 PyTorch-to-CUDA/DSL problems across 4 levels, with its own `n_correctness`-trial allclose-style eval. | Used as a second, independent corpus/kernel-format target (Section 5) -- not a competing checker, an established benchmark to validate generalization against. |
| **SOL-ExecBench** (NVIDIA, arXiv 2603.19173) | Performance/roofline benchmark (235 problems, 124 real models), correctness-gated but scored on proximity to a hardware Speed-of-Light target, Blackwell(B200)-specific. | Investigated, not integrated -- needs B200-class hardware (unavailable) and a from-scratch adapter for its bespoke JSON problem format; also a performance benchmark with correctness as a gate, not a competing correctness oracle. |
| **CodeHacker** (arXiv 2602.20213) | LLM adversarial counterexample search that filters "lucky" submissions passing weak tests, for competitive programming. | Methodological precedent for this project's own LLM-driven adversarial mutant search (Section 6) -- same "hit invariant" idea, different domain (kernels vs. competitive programming). |
| **Kernel Contracts** (arXiv 2604.22032), **ProofWright** (arXiv 2511.12294) | A metamorphic-relation specification language, and agentic formal verification (SMT/Rocq) for CUDA safety/equivalence, respectively. | Related work, not directly benchmarked -- different techniques (specification-only; formal proof) rather than empirical multi-layer testing. |

## 4. Benchmark 1: TritonBench (29 operators, 40 mutants)

### Methodology

Built from `TritonBench/reference/` (29 real, hand-written Triton
kernels) and `TritonBench/cheating/` (40 deliberately-buggy variants, 1-4
per operator, each a real `@triton.jit` kernel with one specific
injected bug). For each mutant: run the reference candidate through each
system 5 times (measuring false-positive rate) and the mutant candidate
once (measuring catch rate). Systems compared: naive `allclose`,
`autokernel_gate`, `gpuemu` (2 strategies), `propilot`, and this
checker's full pipeline plus 3 single-layer ablations.

### Results

| System | Catch rate | False positive rate | Mean latency (ms/check) |
|---|---|---|---|
| allclose | 57% | 0% | 0.10 |
| autokernel_gate | 68% | 18% | 4.4 |
| gpuemu (boundary_shape) | 65% | 0% | 4.0 |
| gpuemu (adversarial_value) | 82% | **82%** | 1.2 |
| propilot | 10% | 0% | 0.4 |
| **your_checker (full)** | **100%** | **0%** | 35.4 |
| your_checker (numeric only) | 100% | 0% | 22.7 |
| your_checker (algebraic only) | 45% | 0% | 3.6 |
| your_checker (structural only) | 10% | 0% | 4.9 |

### What each row means

- **allclose (57%/0%)**: the naive baseline -- one random input, fixed
  tolerance. Misses over 40% of real bugs by design; can't false-positive
  since it's just comparing a kernel to itself when testing the reference.
- **autokernel_gate (68%/18%)**: the strongest published numeric-only
  gate, and it rejects the *correct* reference kernel 18% of the time --
  traced to its fixed-tolerance adversarial-stability stage (extreme
  input magnitudes combined with a tolerance that doesn't scale) and its
  bitwise determinism check (which false-positives on `frobenius_norm`'s
  legitimate atomic-add reduction, whose accumulation order isn't
  associative in floating point).
- **gpuemu adversarial_value (82%/82%)**: deliberately reproduces the
  paper's own reported weakness -- injecting NaN/Inf and comparing with a
  naive (non-`equal_nan`) validator. High catch rate, but the FP rate is
  just as high because a correct kernel legitimately propagating NaN
  through a reduction still trips a NaN != NaN comparison. Not a fair
  system to deploy, but faithful evidence of the mechanism.
- **propilot (10%/0%)**: only 5 of 29 operators have an applicable
  generic skeleton (identity for matmul; permutation-invariance /
  decomposition / scalar-linearity for the 4 pure reductions) -- the
  other 24 trivially "pass" every mutant since nothing tests them. Low
  catch rate is the honest cost of 4 hand-picked skeletons vs. Propilot's
  real 212-operator LLM-authored library, not a flaw in the
  re-implementation.
- **your_checker (full) -- 100%/0%**: catches every mutant, never
  rejects the reference. `numeric only` alone matches this exactly --
  worth being upfront that on this corpus, the adaptive-tolerance numeric
  layer is doing essentially all the catching; structural/algebraic add
  precision and defense-in-depth, not additional recall, here.
- **Latency**: the one real cost. Full checker is ~8x slower than
  `autokernel_gate`, ~9x slower than `gpuemu boundary_shape`. A real
  tradeoff, not hidden.
  > **Caveat (2026-08-20): these ratios come from mean latency, which §8.1.1
  > shows is unreliable — re-derive from p50 before citing.** Both survive
  > the check, but only on p50. On 2026-08-20 p50s: **11.1x** vs
  > `autokernel_gate` and **8.4x** vs `gpuemu boundary_shape` — close to the
  > figures above. On that run's *means* the same comparisons give 37.3x and
  > **2.1x**: the `gpuemu boundary_shape` claim would be wrong by a factor of
  > four, because that baseline has a 21.9x tail of its own and comparing two
  > tail-contaminated means understates the gap. Direction and rough magnitude
  > hold; the specific multiples should be restated from p50.
  >
  > **Second, independent confound (2026-08-20):** these multiples also carry
  > JIT-compile cost attributed by dict order — `full` runs 8th and pays
  > compiles the baselines never trigger. See the boxed note in §8.1. Catch
  > and FP rates are unaffected; the latency multiples are unverified until
  > the cache-warming fix lands.

## 5. Benchmark 2: KernelBench (15 operators, 15 mutants)

### Methodology

KernelBench (ICML'25) tasks LLMs with writing CUDA kernels for PyTorch
reference `Model` classes, evaluated via a `ModelNew` candidate
convention (`torch.utils.cpp_extension.load_inline`-compiled CUDA,
matching KernelBench's own example format exactly). Built 15 hand-authored
(reference, mutant) CUDA kernel pairs across KernelBench's real Level-1
problem set (matmul, softmax, layernorm, and 12 elementwise activations),
using deliberately small shapes (KernelBench's own defaults are sized for
perf benchmarking, not a fast correctness smoke test). Every kernel's math
was hand-verified against PyTorch before writing the CUDA translation --
this machine has no GPU, so nothing here was compiled or executed locally;
only reasoned about and Python-verified, then run for real on Colab.

Checked via `verification/kernel_adapter.py` -- a **separate, leaner**
pipeline from the TritonBench one above (Section 5.4 explains why that
matters).

### Results

| Operator | Mutant | Reference FP? | Mutant caught? |
|---|---|---|---|
| softmax | first_tile | No | Yes |
| swish | linear_sigmoid_approx | No | Yes |
| gelu | sigmoid_approx | No | Yes |
| matmul | partial_k_reduct | No | Yes |
| relu | leaky | No | Yes |
| sigmoid | unstable_exp | No | **No (miss)** |
| tanh | hard_approx | No | Yes |
| layernorm | skip_mean_subtract | No | Yes |
| leaky_relu | wrong_slope | No | Yes |
| elu | missing_minus_one | No | Yes |
| selu | missing_scale | No | Yes |
| hardsigmoid | wrong_divisor | No | Yes |
| softplus | linear_approx | No | Yes |
| softsign | wrong_denom | No | Yes |
| hardtanh | wrong_bounds | No | Yes |

**14/15 caught, 0/15 false positives.**

### 5.3 -- the one miss, explained (not hidden)

`sigmoid/unstable_exp`'s bug (`exp(x)/(1+exp(x))` instead of the stable
`1/(1+exp(-x))`) only diverges once `exp(x)` overflows fp32, around
`x > 88`. The base random input (~±16) never reaches that. Section 4's
`autokernel_gate`/`gpuemu` have adversarial-stability stages that push to
extreme magnitude and would catch this; **this KernelBench pathway
doesn't have an equivalent large-magnitude probe** -- see 5.4. Confirmed
by direct calculation (not guessed): at base scale both formulas agree to
5 decimal places; at 1e4 scale the mutant produces NaN, the reference
doesn't. A real, traced, fixable gap -- not a mystery result.

### 5.4 -- why this pathway is leaner than TritonBench's

`verification/kernel_adapter.py` (KernelBench path) and
`verification/checker.py` (TritonBench path) are **two different
pipelines**, not the same checker pointed at two formats:

| Check | TritonBench path (`checker.py`) | KernelBench path (`kernel_adapter.py`) |
|---|---|---|
| `allclose`, `perturbation_tolerance` (adaptive), `determinism`, `nan_inf`, shape/dtype | Yes | Yes |
| Per-operator algebraic properties (where a spec exists) | Yes | Yes |
| AST structural checks (ghost-optimization, timing manipulation, partial computation) | Yes -- detects Triton's `kernel[grid](...)` launch syntax | **No** -- not invoked at all in this pathway |
| `cross_shape`, `weight_magnitude` (large-magnitude adversarial probe) | Yes | **No** -- this is why 5.3's miss happens |
| Tile-coverage sentinel patching | Yes (softmax) | No |

This is a real, honest architectural gap discovered during this work, not
a design decision -- the fix (add a `weight_magnitude`-equivalent probe to
`kernel_adapter.py`) is understood and scoped, just not yet done.

## 6. Adversarial search: LLM-driven mutant discovery

### Methodology

A separate system (`verification/adversarial_search/`) where an LLM
(Claude Sonnet 4.6) proposes adversarial inputs, iterating against
feedback, trying to find a **"hit"**: an input where (1) the reference
passes the full checker, (2) at least one mutant fails the checker, and
(3) that same mutant *also passes naive allclose*. Condition 3 is what
makes a hit meaningful -- it proves the checker caught something naive
testing would have missed, discovered automatically rather than
hand-designed.

### Results (9 operators run this session's follow-up work)

| Operator | Result | Proposals | Time | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| softmax | HIT | 12 | 104s | first_tile + wrong_reduction both caught |
| layernorm | HIT | 12 | 154s | ignore_gamma_beta + wrong_variance both caught |
| matmul | HIT | 4 | 59s | skip_boundary caught via fp16 accumulator overflow |
| flash_attention | HIT | 12 | 178s | approx_denom caught via rescaling-tile trick |
| rmsnorm | HIT | 4 | 54s | ignore_gamma caught via mean(x^2) vs mean(|x|) divergence |
| gelu | HIT | 18 | 83s | sigmoid_approx caught at x in [2,4] divergence zone |
| instancenorm | HIT | 18 | 69s | skip_eps caught via near-zero-variance input |
| argmax | HIT | 24 | 76s | tiebreak caught via unique-max-per-row input |
| causal_flash_attention | **NO HIT** | 120 | 348s | exhausted budget -- see below |

**8/9 hits.** The `causal_flash_attention` non-hit exhausted its full
proposal budget (120, vs. ~4-24 for the others) without finding an input
where reference passes and mutant fails via a real gap -- likely a
harder search space for this specific `wrong_causal_mask` bug pattern
given the budget used, not yet root-caused further.

## 7. Checks registry: SOTA common ground

`benchmarks/sota_checks_registry.py` (+ generated
`benchmarks/SOTA_CHECKS_REGISTRY.md`) -- a single catalog of all 34
checks across the 5 methods above, each tagged by layer
(structural/numeric/algebraic), methodology (fixed vs. adaptive
tolerance, AST vs. black-box), and operator scope. Built because this
information was scattered across separate files with no side-by-side
view.

**What it's for**: identifies exactly which checks are unique to this
project (adaptive tolerance, AST analysis, tile-coverage sentinels -- no
SOTA method has any of these) vs. which SOTA methods have that this
checker doesn't have a generic equivalent for (gpuemu's automatic
fuzzing, propilot's skeleton-matching -- both hand-authored per-operator
here, not automatic). Sets up a concrete **integration method** direction:
run the hand-authored checks where they exist, fall back to generic
fuzzing/skeleton-matching where they don't -- plausibly beating every
individual method, not yet built.

latency, but with zero false positives instead of 18% **on corpus
inputs** (see the scope note in §1).

Structural (4.9ms) and algebraic (3.6ms) are individually cheap; nearly
all of `full`'s cost is the numeric layer's adaptive perturbation check
(20 perturbation samples per call), cross-shape sweep (5 shapes), and
per-operator adversarial battery (~5 inputs/operator) -- more actual
kernel invocations per check than the other two layers combined.

#### 8.1.3 -- Perturbation batching: Stage A worked, Stage B did not (2026-08-21)

`check_perturbation_tolerance` ran 20 samples in a Python loop, each costing a
kernel launch **and** a `.item()` GPU sync. ~159 calls per 40-mutant pass, so
roughly 19,000 launch+sync pairs per full benchmark.

**Whole-system latency was unmeasurable this run and is not reported.** Systems
that *cannot* be affected by either change drifted **−30.2% to +2.2%**
within-session (`autokernel_gate` −30.2%, `algebraic only` −23.1%). Any
whole-run figure here would be noise. The per-check `duration_ms` added in #7a
isolates the effect instead:

| | n | median | total | vs prior |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| baseline | 159 | 4.22ms | 701ms | — |
| **Stage A** (drop per-sample `.item()`) | 159 | **3.33ms** | **559ms** | **−21.1% median, −20.2% total** |
| **Stage A+B** (also batch kernel calls) | 159 | 3.33ms | 601ms | **+0.0% median, +7.4% total** |

Control — non-perturbation checks in the same system, same runs — moved −3.6%
then +8.0%. So **Stage A's −20% clears the control drift; Stage B's +7.4% is
the control drift**, and its median change is exactly zero.

**Stage A is the whole win, and the reason is instructive.** The syncs, not the
launches, were the serializer: `.item()` per iteration stalled the pipeline 20
times, so the launches could never overlap. Remove the syncs and the launches
pipeline on their own — leaving batching nothing to recover. The plan guessed
the sync was "probably the bigger cost"; it was effectively the only cost.

**Stage B is implemented, verified bit-identical, and shows no measured
benefit.** It is gated per-operator (`KernelSpec.batch_samples`, default off)
because batching a global-reduction operator silently loosens the tolerance
rather than erroring — measured on a `frobenius_norm` stand-in, `adaptive_tol`
went 0.001218 → **0.778163, 639x looser**, no exception raised. Carrying that
surface for a zero-measured gain is a poor trade; **recommend defaulting
`batch_samples` to off** unless a case appears (a much larger `n_samples`, or
operators where launch count genuinely dominates).

Verdicts identical across all four `your_checker` systems in all three runs.

  The old figure compared allclose's numpy comparison against the checker's
  whole pipeline.
- **The heavy tail was the compile bill.** `full`'s mean/p50 fell 5.6x -> 1.2x
  once warmed. What remains (`autokernel_gate (faithful)`, p90 326ms against a
  11.85ms p50) is a genuine tail worth investigating on its own.
- **Structural is no longer the cheapest checker layer** -- algebraic (1.17ms)
  now undercuts it (3.97ms). Cold measurement had them the other way round.

#### 8.1.1 -- Latency is heavy-tailed; read p50, not the mean (2026-08-20)

> **Superseded by §8.1.2.** The heavy tail described below was largely Triton
> JIT compilation; warming the cache collapsed `your_checker (full)`'s mean/p50
> from 5.6x to 1.2x. Kept because the mean-vs-p50 argument stands on its own
> and the cold figures are the evidence for the compile finding.


**Every latency figure in the table above is a mean, and the mean is the
wrong statistic for this distribution.** Measured on the 2026-08-20
TritonBench run (240 trials per system, percentiles by linear
interpolation so p50 = median):

| System | p50 | p90 | p99 | mean | mean/p50 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| allclose | 0.10 | 0.18 | 0.31 | 0.12 | 1.2x |
| autokernel_gate | 2.90 | 7.48 | 21.96 | 4.85 | 1.7x |
| gpuemu (boundary_shape) | 3.87 | 309.26 | 895.88 | 84.76 | **21.9x** |
| gpuemu (adversarial_value) | 1.08 | 1.37 | 1.73 | 1.08 | 1.0x |
| autokernel_gate (faithful) | 15.03 | 1126.75 | 8284.61 | 503.77 | **33.5x** |
| autokernel_gate (faithful, rtol=0) | 12.06 | 358.75 | 547.79 | 67.09 | 5.6x |
| **your_checker (full)** | **32.34** | 472.57 | 2248.94 | 180.64 | **5.6x** |
| your_checker (numeric only) | 15.66 | 34.80 | 1330.07 | 64.33 | 4.1x |
| your_checker (algebraic only) | 1.04 | 21.78 | 167.10 | 7.79 | 7.5x |
| your_checker (structural only) | 3.50 | 4.90 | 11.21 | 3.93 | 1.1x |

For `your_checker (full)`, the slowest **10 of 240 trials (4%) are 49% of
total time**, and the slowest 24 (10%) are 73%. Cost concentrates by
operator: flash_attention 18.3%, avg_pool1d 9.7%, matmul 7.7%, avg_pool2d
7.2%, layernorm 6.5% -- top 10 operators are 71.3% of the total. The
single worst entry, `avg_pool1d/wrong_divisor` at 4218ms, is ~10% of the
whole run on its own.

**The strongest evidence that the mean is unstable is the two runs
disagreeing.** Catch and false-positive rates were reproducible across
runs, but the means moved sharply: `your_checker (full)` 35.4 -> 180.64ms,
and `gpuemu (boundary_shape)` 4.0 -> 84.76ms (**21x**). The p50s are the
stable quantity. A metric that swings 21x between runs while the thing it
measures has not changed is not measuring what the table claims.

**Consequences for the claims above:**

- The `numeric only` vs `full` finding **holds** -- identical catch and FP
  rates in this run too -- but the speed ratio depends on the statistic:
  1.6x on the old means, 2.8x on this run's means, **2.1x on p50**.
- "Structural and algebraic are individually cheap" **holds**, and is
  strengthened: `structural only` has a mean/p50 of 1.1x, i.e. essentially
  no tail. The tail lives in the numeric and algebraic layers.
- Whether the tail is intrinsic (more kernel invocations on larger inputs)
  or a fixable defect is **not yet determined** -- deliberately not
  guessed at here. `results_raw.json` records per-check outcomes but not
  per-check timing, so attributing the 4.2s entry needs profiling that has
  not been done.

`harness.summarize()` now emits `p50/p90/p99/max_latency_ms` alongside
`mean_latency_ms`; the mean is retained unchanged so earlier runs stay
comparable.

**Note this is a *separate* problem from the two confounds in §8.1's boxed
note.** Mean-vs-p50 is about which statistic summarises a distribution;
measurement order and timer scope are about whether the systems are being
measured comparably at all. Fixing the statistic does not fix the
comparison — and the 2026-08-20 root-cause found that **58% of `full`'s
measured time is JIT compilation**, which is also what produces the heavy
tail described above. The tail and the confound are the same phenomenon
seen from two angles.

### 8.2 -- Precision / Recall / F1 (TritonBench, standard classification framing)

Treating "mutant" as the positive class (real bug) and "reference" as
negative: catch rate above is Recall; derived Precision = TP/(TP+FP)
using the reported rates against 40 mutant trials / 200 reference trials
per system (approximate -- computed from rounded published percentages,
not independently re-measured):

| System | Precision | Recall | F1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| allclose | 100% | 57% | 73% |
| propilot | 100% | 10% | 18% |
| your_checker (structural only) | 100% | 10% | 18% |
| your_checker (algebraic only) | 100% | 45% | 62% |
| gpuemu (boundary_shape) | 100% | 65% | 79% |
| autokernel_gate | ~43% | 68% | ~53% |
| gpuemu (adversarial_value) | **~17%** | 82% | **~28%** |
| your_checker (numeric only / full) | 100% | 100% | **100%** |

**Why this framing matters**: `gpuemu (adversarial_value)`'s headline 82%
catch rate looks strong until precision is computed -- at ~17% precision,
roughly 5 of every 6 "failures" it reports are false alarms on correct
code, not real bugs. `autokernel_gate` similarly drops to ~43% precision.
Precision/recall together are a fairer comparison than either number
alone, and this is the clearest way to show it.

### 8.3 -- Statistical confidence on the 0% false-positive claim

"0% FP" from a finite number of trials isn't the same as "provably zero."
Using the rule of three (a standard approximation: zero events observed
in *n* trials bounds the true rate at roughly 3/*n* with 95% confidence):
the most recent clean TritonBench run had 0 false positives across 200
reference trials (40 mutants x 5 trials each) for the full checker -->
**true false-positive rate is below ~1.5% at 95% confidence** from that
run alone. This result was also confirmed clean (0% FP) across multiple
independent full benchmark runs earlier in this session, which tightens
that bound further, though an exact aggregate trial count across every
run isn't claimed here to avoid overstating precision.

**What this bound does and does not cover.** The rule-of-three bound
applies to the population it was sampled from: **reference trials on the
fixed corpus inputs** produced by `spec.make_inputs`. It is a statement
about that input distribution, not about correct kernels in general.
Drawing from a different distribution gives a very different rate.

Measured on 2026-08-20, on the same checker: an 80-proposal adversarial
search against `causal_flash_attention` — where inputs are generated by
an LLM specifically to stress the checker — saw the **reference kernel
fail 29 of 80 proposals (36%)**: 20x `kernel_executed`, 9x `nan_inf` +
`dtype_preserved`. The 20 `kernel_executed` failures are a documented
Layer-1 unsoundness on shift-invariant operators
(`benchmarks/CHECK_ABLATION_FINDINGS.md` §3.0), not noise.

These two figures are **not contradictory** — they measure different
input distributions, and the ~1.5% bound above remains valid for the one
it was computed on. But "0% false positives" stated without qualification
invites the reading "on any correct kernel", and that reading is
contradicted by the data above for at least one operator family. Cite the
0% figure **with its distribution attached**.

#### 8.3.1 -- Post-fix re-measurement (2026-08-21)

`check_kernel_executed` was fixed on 2026-08-21 (probe ladder; the
deterministic evidence is a 25/25 replay against real Triton kernels with
zero corpus verdict changes). The search above was then re-run under the
**identical** configuration -- `causal_flash_attention`, `--strategy beam
--workers 4 --max-iter 20`, same model.

> **This section was corrected on 2026-08-21 after the failures were
> diagnosed.** It first reported a **17.1% false-positive rate**. That figure
> was **wrong** -- it counted 12 crashes on *invalid input* as checker false
> positives. The mechanism is written out below rather than only the corrected
> number, because a bare number swap gives a reader no way to judge whether it
> will move again.

**Headline: on valid inputs the checker's false-positive rate is 0 of 58 =
0.0%**, down from 20 of 71 = 28.2% before the fix.

| | 2026-08-20 | 2026-08-21 |
|---|---|---|
| reference executions | 80 | 74 |
| infrastructure timeouts (excluded) | 0 | 4 |
| evaluable | 80 | 70 |
| out-of-domain proposals (invalid input -- see below) | 9 | 12 |
| **in-domain evaluable** | **71** | **58** |
| **false positives on in-domain input** | **20 (28.2%)** | **0 (0.0%)** |
| -- `kernel_executed` | 20 | **0** |

**`kernel_executed` no longer false-positives**: 0 of 58, down from 20 of 71.
Nothing else took its place.

**The 12 out-of-domain proposals, and why they are not false positives.**
16.2% of the run (12 of 74 proposals; 17.1% of the 70 evaluable) proposed
inputs the reference kernel **cannot accept**. `causal_flash_attention` has two
hard preconditions, both at source in
`TritonBench/reference/causal_flash_attention.py`:

- **rank exactly 2.** The wrapper does `N, D = Q.shape`; its docstring reads
  *"Q, K, V: (N, D) -- single sequence, no batch/head dimension."*
- **D a power of two.** `D` is passed as a `tl.constexpr` into
  `tl.arange(0, D)`, and Triton requires a power-of-two range.

Every one of the 12 violates one of these, in two clean classes:

| class | n | exception raised | violation |
|---|---:|---|---|
| A | 7 | `arange's range must be a power of 2` | D = 48 or 33 -- fails at **compile** time, the kernel never runs |
| B | 5 | `too many values to unpack (expected 2)` | rank-3 / rank-4 input |

**These crashes were reported as `nan_inf` and `dtype_preserved` failures.**
Both `check_nan_inf` and `check_dtype_preserved`
(`verification/layer1_structural/runtime_guards.py`) wrap their kernel call in
`try/except` and return `(False, "Kernel raised an exception: ...")` for **any**
exception. So the two checks **conflate "the kernel ran and produced a bad
number" with "the kernel could not run on this input at all."** Not one of the
12 contains a NaN, an Inf, or a dtype change. Scored against the *reference*
kernel, that conflation turns "the harness fed a correct kernel an input outside
its domain" into "the correct kernel failed the checker" -- a false positive
that never happened.

**The separation is exact, in both directions**, which is why the reading is
not a judgement call:

| 2026-08-21 | checker FAIL | checker pass |
|---|---:|---:|
| in-domain | **0** | 58 |
| out-of-domain | **12** | 0 |

No in-domain input failed; no out-of-domain input passed. The same rule applied
to the 2026-08-20 run accounts for all 9 of its `nan_inf` failures and leaves
exactly the 20 `kernel_executed` false positives as the in-domain residual.

**A superseded reading, recorded so it is not repeated.** These failures were
first characterised as tracking *non-power-of-two shapes* (78% of them carried
such a dim, against 41% of other proposals). That correlation is real but
explains **only class A**. Two of the five class-B cases are entirely
powers of two; they fail on **rank**. A fix aimed at odd shapes would have
addressed 7 of 12 and none of the rank cases -- the same trap as the original
§3.0 diagnosis, whose recommended fix rescued 0 of 20.

**What this does and does not license.** The 0.0% is measured on 58 in-domain
adversarial proposals for **one operator**, so the §1 scope caveat still stands:
these are adversarially-generated inputs for `causal_flash_attention`, not a
claim about every operator. What changed is that the adversarial and corpus
figures no longer disagree -- the earlier tension between "0% on the corpus" and
a double-digit adversarial rate was an artifact of counting crashes-on-invalid-
input as false positives.

**Two defects this exposed, both open and neither fixed here:**

1. The exception-conflation in `check_nan_inf` / `check_dtype_preserved`
   described above.
2. `validate_proposal` (`verification/adversarial_search/schemas.py`) checks
   tensor **keys** and fill validity only -- it has no rank or shape check, so
   out-of-domain proposals reach execution. All 12 crashed the mutant as well as
   the reference, producing no comparison at all: **16% of the search budget
   spent on proposals that could not yield information**, one LLM call and two
   subprocess spawns each. The prompt does state both constraints
   (`OPERATOR_CONTEXT`), emphatically for rank; the model violates them anyway.

**Unchanged caveats on the re-run itself.** The search is LLM-driven and
non-deterministic, so this corroborates a *rate*; the deterministic proof of the
fix remains the 25/25 real-Triton replay. Worker w0 died at iteration 13 on
unparseable model JSON, which is why there are 74 proposals rather than 80; the
4 timeouts are `TimeoutError` at the 30s default -- infrastructure, excluded
above rather than counted as false positives.

Artifacts: `adversarial_results/cfa_rerun_postfix_2026-08-21/`.

## 9. Bugs found and fixed this session (rigor evidence)

Every number above reflects a checker that had 7 real, confirmed bugs
found and fixed along the way -- not a checker that happened to work on
the first try. Each was found by running against a real target, tracing
an unexpected result to its root cause, fixing narrowly, and verifying
the fix didn't just paper over the bug:

1. **`layernorm`/`rmsnorm` precision-coercion check** (`verification/specs/`)
   was comparing a candidate called with *real* gamma/beta against a
   reference computed with *identity* gamma/beta -- false-positived on
   any non-identity affine params. Algebraic-layer FP rate: 15% -> 0%.
2. **`check_determinism`** used bitwise equality, which false-positives
   on `frobenius_norm`'s legitimate atomic-add reduction (floating-point
   addition isn't associative across concurrent thread blocks). Switched
   to a tight adaptive tolerance; verified a real race still fails hard.
3. **`check_monotonicity`** (softmax, log_softmax) compared exact
   `argsort` index permutations -- brittle near floating-point ties.
   Switched to a tolerance-based ordering check; verified near-ties pass,
   genuinely scrambled output still fails.
4. **matmul's `scalar_associativity`/`distributivity`** checks used too
   tight a tolerance for ordinary fp32 cancellation at scale -- false
   positived on a correct reference matmul (~0.0012 vs. atol=1e-4).
   Loosened with a principled floor+relative tolerance.
5. **`kernel_adapter.py` never moved tensors/models to the GPU** -- a
   CUDA-kernel candidate launched against CPU-resident memory, causing
   illegal-memory-access errors and garbage output on *both* reference
   and mutant. First KernelBench run: 4/4 reference false positives.
6. **`kernel_adapter.py`'s known-operator property dispatch** passed a
   1-tuple to single-tensor-spec property functions expecting a bare
   tensor -- crashed every softmax/swish/gelu-family algebraic check.
7. **KernelBench operators without a registry entry got silently
   misidentified as softmax** by a shape-guessing heuristic (any
   single-2D-tensor call looks softmax-shaped to it), asserting
   "rows sum to 1" against relu/sigmoid/tanh/layernorm outputs. Fixed by
   registering every operator (including under non-colliding keys for
   ones with no real per-operator spec) so the heuristic never fires.
   Reference FP rate: 4/8 -> 0/8 on that run.

## 10. Honest limitations (not swept under the rug)

- **KernelBench pathway lacks structural (AST) and large-magnitude
  numeric checks** that the TritonBench pathway has (Section 5.4) --
  directly caused the one KernelBench miss.
- **Propilot re-implementation covers 5 of 29 operators**, vs. the real
  Propilot's 212 TVM operators -- an honest scope limitation, not a bug.
- **Adversarial search covers 9 of 21 wired operators** so far; 8 more
  operators (`cross_entropy`, `groupnorm`, the 6 pooling ops) are
  structurally excluded from the search system entirely -- its
  `InputProposal` schema has no field for int64 class-index targets or
  int hyperparameters like `num_groups`/`kernel_size`.
- **SOL-ExecBench and KernelBench Levels 2-4 not attempted** -- hardware
  (B200) and format-adapter requirements put them out of scope for this
  round.
- **Latency**: full checker is ~5-9x slower than the fastest SOTA
  baselines on TritonBench. A real, undisputed cost of the extra rigor.
  > **Caveat (2026-08-20): mean-derived; re-derive from p50 — see §8.1.1.**
  > The "~5-9x" band understates the spread once measured on p50: 8.4x vs
  > `gpuemu boundary_shape`, 11.1x vs `autokernel_gate`, 29.9x vs `gpuemu
  > adversarial_value`. The cost is real and undisputed, but the range should
  > be restated per-baseline rather than as a single band, and from p50 rather
  > than mean.
  >
  > **Second, independent confound (2026-08-20):** `full` runs 8th in the
  > systems dict and absorbs Triton JIT compilation the baselines never
  > trigger — 58% of its measured time on the 2026-08-20 run. See §8.1's boxed
  > note. **Catch rate and FP rate are unaffected and still hold; only the
  > latency multiples are unverified** pending the cache-warming fix.
- **This machine has no GPU** -- every CUDA/Triton result in this
  document was produced on Colab, not locally; local work was limited to
  writing, hand-verifying math against PyTorch, and static analysis
  before handoff.
