# GPU and ML Kernel Correctness Oracles: Novelty Assessment for a Three-Layer Checker with LLM Adversarial Mutant Search
## (Literature Sweep through Aug 2026)

## TL;DR
- **The single most novelty-threatening new find is Propilot / "Tensor Algebraic Property Skeletons" (arXiv 2606.06747, UC Riverside/UCLA/Tulane), an implemented, LLM-driven algebraic/metamorphic property-based testing framework that encodes reusable tensor-algebra property skeletons (softmax shift-invariance, relu idempotence, add associativity, reduction decomposition) into 4,579 executable property-based tests over 212 TVM operators** — it directly overlaps your middle "algebraic property" layer, but targets the TVM DL compiler rather than raw Triton kernels, leaving your kernel-level instantiation defensible.
- Your "hit invariant" and 29-operator mutant-corpus framing remain **substantially novel**: no found work combines (a) a three-layer structural+numeric+algebraic checker, (b) an LLM-driven adversarial search for allclose-passing-but-wrong mutants, and (c) a reference/mutant-pair corpus built to benchmark the oracle itself. The closest analog, CodeHacker (arXiv 2602.20213), does exactly this "hit"-style adversarial counterexample search but for competitive-programming code, not kernels.
- Several 2026 preprints now independently attack the "allclose is a lying oracle" thesis (Sarkar's gpuemu family, Kernel Contracts, ProofWright, KernelBench-Verified). The framing is getting crowded, so the paper should foreground the *automated LLM adversarial mutant-generation loop* and the *algebraic-property layer applied to Triton* as the crisp differentiators.

## Key Findings

**Most overlapping new works (rank-ordered by threat to novelty):**
1. **Propilot (Tensor Algebraic Property Skeletons, 2606.06747)** — algebraic-property PBT for tensor operators; overlaps the algebraic layer directly, but compiler-scoped (TVM), not kernel-scoped, and with no AST layer or adversarial mutant search.
2. **CodeHacker (2602.20213)** — LLM adversarial counterexample search that filters "lucky" allclose-passing-but-buggy submissions; overlaps the adversarial-search engine and the "illusion of correctness" framing, but the domain is competitive programming, not GPU kernels.
3. **Characterizing Real-World Bugs in Tile Programs (2605.19652)** — uses schedule-invariance and padding-equivalence metamorphic oracles for tile/Triton programs; overlaps the metamorphic-oracle idea at the kernel level, but is an empirical bug study, not an oracle system.
4. **ProofWright (2511.12294)** — agentic formal verification (VerCors/Rocq) of CUDA; overlaps the "beyond allclose" goal via formal methods, orthogonal to property/numeric testing.

**Corroborating context works:** AutoKernel five-stage verification, Kaizen metamorphic fuzzing, Static PTX Metrics, Dr. Kernel/KernelGym, NVIDIA automotive multi-layer verification, MT-DLComp/HirGen/OATest/ORTHRUS (DL-compiler testing lineage), GPU-NBDetect.

## Details

### 1. Algebraic / property-based oracles for tensor operators and kernels

**Propilot — "Tensor Algebraic Property Skeletons: Amplifying Property-Based Testing for AI Compilers" (arXiv 2606.06747).** Authors: Yuxin Qiu (UC Riverside), Ben Limpanukorn, Seongmin Lee (UCLA), Jiyuan Wang (Tulane), Qian Zhang (UC Riverside), Miryung Kim (UCLA). This is the most methodologically on-point prior art for your algebraic layer. It defines reusable "property skeletons" — commutativity, associativity, identity, permutation invariance, geometric decomposition, decomposition+idempotence — each carrying operator-applicability rules and an oracle template (exact-equality or AllClose). It is described as "an LLM-driven agentic property-based testing framework for DL compilers" (using GPT-5.5) that "produces 4,579 generated property-based tests and reduces estimated redundancy to about 30% and invalid generated tests to 0%," over 212 TVM operators, explicitly including softmax shift-invariance, relu idempotence, add associativity/commutativity, and reduction decomposition. Its measured failure breakdown is telling: "50% are semantic inconsistencies between tensor math and compiler implementation, 25% are numerical instabilities." **Comparison to your project:** this is essentially your middle "algebraic property" layer, already implemented and evaluated — but on the TVM compiler's operator set, not on hand-written or LLM-generated Triton kernels, and without a structural (AST) layer, an adversarial mutant-generation loop, or a "hit invariant" evaluation methodology. **Threat: HIGH on the algebraic-layer sub-claim specifically. You must cite and differentiate directly, and reframe your algebraic layer's novelty as "applied to Triton kernels as part of a three-layer checker + adversarial search," not "algebraic properties as kernel oracles" in the abstract.**

**Kernel Contracts (arXiv 2604.22032, already known to you)** independently proposes metamorphic/algebraic relations (associativity, idempotence, determinism, permutation equivariance, shift invariance) as one of its oracle modes, stating that for such algebraic properties "no reference computation is needed in principle, but the tolerance must be explicitly qualified for floating-point execution." It positions itself against Sakana's robust-kbench explicitly: robust-kbench "documents LLM-generated kernels passing tests while silently failing; their response is harder tests. Our response is complementary: specify the contract the tests enforce." This strengthens the case that the algebraic-oracle idea is "in the air" as of 2026, but Kernel Contracts remains a specification-language position paper, not an implemented, benchmarked oracle.

**Negative result (important):** Sarkar's own "Test-Input Generation for Tensor Programs" (arXiv 2606.27396) explicitly states that only "a few works derive metamorphic relations for specific operators (e.g., softmax shift-invariance)" and that the practice is not systematic. This is direct textual evidence that an implemented, systematic algebraic-property oracle *specifically for GPU/Triton kernels* remains a near-open niche as of August 2026 — which is favorable to your positioning.

### 2. Adversarial / guided search for correctness-exposing inputs

**CodeHacker (arXiv 2602.20213).** Authors include Jingwei Shi, Xinxiang Yin, Jing Huang, Shengyu Tao, Jinman Zhao (SUFE / Northwestern Polytechnical / Meituan / Univ. of Toronto). An LLM agent acts as a "hacking policy" that searches for failure-inducing counterexamples against specific program submissions, using a calibration phase that refines its own Validator and Checker before attacking contestant code. It introduces **CodeHackerBench**, whose Table 3 "drop quantifies the proportion of 'lucky' submissions that contained latent bugs but passed the original weak tests." **Comparison to your project:** this is the closest structural analog to your LLM-driven adversarial mutant search and "hit invariant" (reference passes / mutant passes weak test / stronger checker fails). The critical difference is domain: CodeHacker generates *inputs* to break competitive-programming solutions, whereas you generate *mutant kernels* and search for inputs where the mutant passes allclose but fails your checker. **Threat: MODERATE — same conceptual engine, different domain; strengthens the case that the mechanism is validated while leaving the GPU-kernel instantiation open. Cite as the methodological precedent for adversarial counterexample search.** Related: Code-A1 (arXiv 2603.15611) co-evolves a Code LLM and a Test LLM adversarially via RL; Codehacks (arXiv 2503.23466) is a Codeforces-derived adversarial-test dataset.

**Test-Input Generation for Tensor Programs / gpuemu (arXiv 2606.27396, Dipankar Sarkar, ASU).** Provides a switchable-knob fuzzer (shape/dtype/value-distribution) over a 26-op Triton+CPU corpus (16 correct controls, 10 seeded buggy variants). Reports that a boundary-shape strategy reaches 78% bug recall at 0% control false-positive, while an adversarial value strategy reaches 99% recall but 94% false-positive because it injects NaN/Inf that cascade through correct kernels and trip the validator's NaN check. Public artifacts at github.com/Skelf-Research/gpuemu and a companion gpuemu-corpus repository. **Comparison:** a reference-oracle + fuzzing approach, and a *companion* to the already-known "Correctness Illusion" paper; it competes directly on the "adversarial inputs for kernels" sub-idea but uses random/boundary fuzzing rather than LLM-guided search, and has no algebraic layer or mutant corpus for benchmarking oracles. **Threat: MODERATE-HIGH on input-generation, LOW on the overall three-layer + LLM-search framing.**

### 3. Kernel-level bug studies and metamorphic oracles

**Characterizing Real-World Bugs in Tile Programs (arXiv 2605.19652).** Authors: Ravishka Rathnasuriya, Zihe Song, Nidhi Majoju, Aaryaa Moharir, Tingxi Li, Wei Yang (UT Dallas), Tao Xie (Peking Univ.). First systematic study of tile-program (Triton-style) codegen bugs: "We examine 401 public bug reports … and identify 301 tile codegen bugs," organized into "a taxonomy of six categories—(1) control flow and scheduling, (2) IR construction and transformation, (3) tile mapping and launch configuration, (4) memory, (5) type and operator handling, and (6) device-specific bugs," and it reports that "Type and operator bugs are among the most frequent causes (48.84%)." It uses metamorphic-style oracles including schedule-invariance and padding-equivalence tests. **Comparison:** provides a real-world bug taxonomy your mutant corpus could be validated against, and independently motivates kernel-level metamorphic oracles, but is a characterization study, not an oracle/checker system. **Threat: LOW-MODERATE (motivational overlap; a valuable corpus/taxonomy to cite).** Note this team overlaps with the already-known UT Dallas "Correct but Slow" authors (Li, Yang).

**Kaizen (arXiv 2607.04058).** Metamorphic fuzzing + differential testing for LLM-translated HPC code (CUDA→OpenMP), 16 scientific applications. Argues that "a program that compiles and passes developer tests may be perfectly correct or completely wrong, and only differential testing with diverse inputs can distinguish between them," and independently exposed latent bugs in developer-written CUDA. **Comparison:** metamorphic+differential oracle, but for cross-model HPC translation, not ML operators. **Threat: LOW.**

### 4. Formal verification and static/structural analysis of kernels

**ProofWright (arXiv 2511.12294).** Authors: Bodhisatwa Chatterjee et al. (Georgia Tech); submitted 15 Nov 2025. Agentic formal verification integrating VerCors (SMT-based deductive verification) and Rocq/Coq operator equivalence. Per the paper, "ProofWright successfully establishes memory and thread-safety guarantees for up to 74% of KernelBench L1 programs and can ascertain semantic equivalence in 14% of KernelBench L1 programs" (plus ~3% partial safety), with baseline CUDA kernels generated by Claude-4-Sonnet and GPT-5-Codex. **Comparison:** a "beyond allclose" approach via formal methods, complementary to your dynamic numeric/algebraic testing (it proves safety and limited equivalence; you catch numerical/semantic bugs by testing). Its annotation-level analysis is closest to your AST layer in spirit. **Threat: LOW-MODERATE (different technique, overlapping motivation; a natural related-work anchor for the "formal" alternative alongside the already-known VOLTA/equivalence-checking paper).**

**Static PTX Metrics Track Structural Kernel Regressions but Miss Semantic Ones (arXiv 2607.02541, Sarkar).** Shows static PTX metrics (registers, spills, instruction counts, local memory) detect structural but not semantic kernel regressions, using the same 9 paired correct/buggy Triton variants. **Comparison:** directly relevant to your AST/structural layer — empirical evidence that structural checks alone miss semantic bugs, motivating your multi-layer design. **Threat: LOW (supports your thesis; cite as motivation).**

**AutoKernel (arXiv 2603.21331).** Autonomous GPU kernel optimization whose harness enforces a five-stage correctness gate: smoke test; shape sweep across 8–10 configs and three dtypes; numerical-stability adversarial inputs (softmax rows of large identical values, matmul extreme dynamic range, near-zero-variance normalization); determinism (bitwise-identical across three runs); and a final stage. **Comparison:** the shape-sweep + adversarial-stability stages resemble parts of your numeric-oracle layer, but there is no algebraic-property layer, no AST layer, and no LLM-driven mutant search. **Threat: MODERATE on the numeric-oracle layer — this is the strongest existing multi-stage numerical gate and should be cited as the state of practice you extend.**

**Dr. Kernel / KernelGym (arXiv 2602.05885).** RL environment for Triton generation with explicit "hacking checks" and execution profiling to prevent reward hacking. **Comparison:** a hardened correctness/anti-cheat harness for training, not a general oracle. **Threat: LOW.**

### 5. Commercial / large-org tooling

**NVIDIA automotive multi-layer verification (Springer, "Ensuring Safety in Automotive Machine Learning Inference").** Describes NVIDIA's DRIVE Orin→Thor transition to an MLIR graph compiler, validated via a multi-layer framework: (1) formal methods (symbolic execution + SMT) for memory/concurrency safety, and (2) AI-driven/LLM testing for functional correctness and numerical accuracy, plus runtime sanitizers on target hardware. **Comparison:** a production instance of the multi-layer philosophy (formal + AI-driven testing) you propose, but proprietary, safety-certification-oriented (ISO 26262), and not operator-algebraic. **Threat: LOW-MODERATE (validates the multi-layer approach commercially; worth acknowledging).**

**Sakana AI robust-kbench (already known; arXiv 2509.14279).** "Towards Robust Agentic CUDA Kernel Benchmarking, Verification, and Optimization" by Robert Tjarko Lange, Qi Sun, Aaditya Prasad, Maxence Faldor, Yujin Tang, David Ha. It "introduce[s] an LLM-based soft-verification workflow, capable of accurately classifying incorrect kernels for compilation, memory access, and numerical correctness (up to 80% accuracy)," using "three tuned LLM verifiers (compilation, memory safety, numerical tolerance)." Its evolutionary optimizer "reliably finds kernels that beat PyTorch eager (up to ~2.5× forward)," and after "excluding all contaminated tasks … the average speedup is reduced from 3.13× to 1.49×" — a concrete reward-hacking correction relevant to your motivation section.

**Hypothesis-torch (github.com/qthequartermasterman/hypothesis-torch).** Community property-based-testing strategies for PyTorch tensors/modules; PyTorch, JAX, and NumPy themselves use Hypothesis. **Comparison:** infrastructure you could build on for the numeric/algebraic layers, not a kernel-correctness system. **Threat: NONE (tooling to reuse).**

### 6. DL-compiler metamorphic-testing lineage (context, not direct competitors)
MT-DLComp (metamorphic testing of DL compilers, 435+ erroneous-compilation inputs across Amazon/Facebook/Microsoft-maintained compilers), HirGen, OATest (58 new TVM/ONNXRuntime bugs, 36 confirmed/fixed), and ORTHRUS (optimization-resistant transformations) establish the metamorphic/differential-testing tradition your algebraic layer inherits — but all target compiler toolchains and whole computation graphs, not individual kernel correctness oracles. Propilot (§1) is the point where this lineage meets operator-level algebraic properties, which is why it is your closest competitor.

### 7. Agentic kernel-generation systems (adjacent, allclose-based)
STARK (arXiv 2510.16996, Juncheng Dong et al., Meta/Duke; v1 Oct 19 2025), CUDA Agent (arXiv 2602.24286), KernelBand (arXiv 2511.18868, using torch.allclose at atol=1e-2, rtol=1e-3), SpecGen (arXiv 2606.17518), CudaForge, AKG, KernelBlaster, and the survey "Towards Automated Kernel Generation in the Era of LLMs" (arXiv 2601.15727) all rely on standard allclose-style correctness gates during generation. They are consumers of a better oracle, not competitors on oracle design — a natural audience/adoption path for your tool.

## Recommendations

1. **Immediately read and directly differentiate against Propilot (2606.06747).** It is the only found work that implements algebraic property skeletons (including softmax shift-invariance) as tensor-operator oracles. Frame your novelty as: (a) applied to *hand-written and LLM-generated Triton kernels* rather than TVM compiler operators; (b) *combined* with an AST/structural layer and a numeric oracle in one checker; (c) *driven by an LLM adversarial mutant-generation loop* with the "hit invariant" as the evaluation target. If you cannot articulate a clean delta on the algebraic layer alone, shift the paper's contribution weight onto the adversarial-search + mutant-corpus methodology.

2. **Position CodeHacker (2602.20213) as the methodological precedent for your adversarial search, and claim the GPU-kernel instantiation as novel.** Adopt its framing of "lucky submissions that passed weak tests" and its calibration-phase idea (refine and validate the checker before trusting its verdicts) — this will preempt reviewer objections that your adversarial loop is unprecedented, while making clear that no one has applied it to kernel mutants.

3. **Benchmark your mutant corpus against real bug taxonomies.** Map your 29-operator mutants onto the six-category taxonomy in the Tile Programs bug study (2605.19652 — note type/operator bugs are 48.84% of real cases) and the seeded transcription patterns in Sarkar's corpus, so reviewers see your mutants are ecologically valid, not synthetic strawmen. This is the strongest defense of your "benchmark-for-benchmarking-oracles" contribution, which nothing found does for GPU kernels.

4. **Contrast the three layers against the closest single-layer baselines in one table:** AST/structural vs. Static-PTX-Metrics (2607.02541); numeric oracle vs. AutoKernel's five-stage gate (2603.21331) and Sarkar's fp64+fuzzing (2606.20128 / 2606.27396); algebraic vs. Propilot (2606.06747) and Kernel Contracts (2604.22032); formal alternatives vs. ProofWright (2511.12294) and VOLTA. A single comparison matrix keyed to these will make the multi-layer contribution legible to reviewers.

5. **Move fast on venue timing.** The "allclose is lying" thesis is being staked out rapidly by multiple 2026 preprints (Sarkar family, Kernel Contracts, KernelBench-Verified at 2607.16241, Propilot). For MLSys 2027, the defensible core is the *automated, LLM-driven discovery of allclose-passing mutants across a broad operator corpus, validated by a three-layer checker* — emphasize the pipeline and the corpus, and treat the "illusion of correctness" observation as established motivation rather than a headline claim.

**Thresholds that change these recommendations:** If Propilot releases a Triton/CUDA-targeted extension before your submission, the algebraic-layer novelty largely collapses and you should pivot the contribution entirely to the adversarial mutant-search + oracle-benchmark corpus. If any Sarkar preprint is accepted at a peer-reviewed venue with an expanded operator set overlapping your 29 ops, cite it as concurrent and stress your LLM-guided (vs. random/boundary) search and your algebraic layer.

## Caveats
- **Source quality:** The most on-topic Triton-oracle works (Sarkar's gpuemu family: 2606.20128, 2606.27396, 2607.02541, plus a tolerance-calibration companion) are single-author, mutually cross-citing, un-peer-reviewed ASU preprints; treat their detection statistics (9/9, 10/10) as self-reported. Kernel Contracts (2604.22032) is a position/specification paper, not a benchmarked system. Propilot (2606.06747) is the most rigorously evaluated (212 ops, 4,579 tests) but compiler-scoped.
- **arXiv ID conventions:** Several IDs here use 2026 YYMM prefixes (e.g., 2602–2607) reflecting the current date of August 2026; some are still v1 preprints subject to revision. STARK (2510.16996) and ProofWright (2511.12294) are 2025.
- **Coverage limits:** The web-search budget was exhausted before I could confirm the MLSys/NeurIPS/ICML/ICLR/ASPLOS/PLDI 2025–2026 accepted-paper lists directly; the venue-specific competitor scan relies on arXiv and secondary indexes. No accepted proceedings paper was found that implements a three-layer structural+numeric+algebraic kernel checker with LLM adversarial mutant search — but absence of evidence here is not proof of absence, and Propilot in particular may be targeting exactly such a venue.
- **CodeHackerBench metric:** the "lucky proportion" is reported per-model in Table 3 of arXiv:2602.20213, not as a single headline scalar; pull that table directly if you need a specific number.
